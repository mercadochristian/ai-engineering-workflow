# Publication boundary

The public lesson is the engineering method. The original project remains private.

## Safe material

- My own explanation of a general workflow or decision.
- An anonymized account that omits identifying product behavior and metadata.
- A newly written synthetic example with invented entities and data.
- A reusable template rewritten without local configuration or private context.

## Material excluded from this repository

- Employer or customer names, proprietary code, schemas, and business rules.
- Tickets, private pull requests, commit identifiers, and internal repository links.
- Hostnames, local absolute paths, account identifiers, and deployment configuration.
- Credentials, environment files, database exports, and production data.
- Raw conversations, private checkpoints, screenshots, recordings, and original logs.

Changing a company name is not sufficient anonymization. Review whether domain
details, dates, counts, or combinations of facts could identify the original work.
Generalize or omit them when they are unnecessary to explain the decision.

Before adding a case study, review both the files and the material Git would
publish. Keep original evidence outside the repository. The ignore file reduces
accidental additions; it is not a guarantee against disclosure.

Do not publish material unless you have the right and authorization to share it.
If ownership is uncertain, write a new generic example instead.
